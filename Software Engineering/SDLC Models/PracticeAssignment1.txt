In this scenario, the project has fixed requirements, strict deadlines, and requires heavy documentation. 
The most suitable SDLC model for such a project is the Waterfall Model.

1. Justification for Waterfall Model:

a) Fixed Requirements:
In Waterfall, requirements are clearly defined at the beginning and remain stable throughout the project.
This matches the scenario where requirements are already fixed.

b) Strict Deadlines:
Waterfall follows a structured and sequential approach, making it easier to plan timelines and meet deadlines.

c) Heavy Documentation:
Each phase (requirement, design, development, testing) is well documented.
Government projects often require detailed reports and approvals, which Waterfall supports.

d) Easy Management:
Since each phase is completed before moving to the next, project tracking and management become simpler.

e) Predictability:
Waterfall provides clear milestones and deliverables, making the project predictable.

------------------------------------------------------------

2. Drawbacks if Agile Model is Used:

a) Frequent Changes:
Agile encourages continuous changes, but in government projects, requirements are fixed.
This can create confusion and unnecessary modifications.

b) Less Documentation:
Agile focuses more on working software than documentation.
This is not suitable for projects requiring detailed records and approvals.

c) Difficulty in Compliance:
Government projects often follow strict rules and standards.
Agile's flexible nature may not align with these regulations.

d) Uncertain Timelines:
Agile works in iterations, making it harder to predict exact completion time.

e) Client Involvement:
Agile requires continuous client interaction.
In government projects, frequent involvement may not always be possible.

------------------------------------------------------------

Conclusion:

Waterfall model is the best choice for this project because it supports fixed requirements, strict deadlines, and heavy documentation. 
Using Agile in such a scenario may lead to confusion, lack of documentation, and difficulty in meeting compliance requirements.
